Hello, everyone, and welcome aboard the Sunshine Express on our journey from London to Naples. I'm Jane Sharpe, the train manager, and I hope you'll all enjoy the trip.

Before we depart, I'd like to tell you a bit about the train and its facilities. We're here on the observation deck, which offers the best views, and directly below us is our leisure centre. There are games machines, a television and a small library. If you've brought a laptop, you can also get onto the Internet here, as it has full Wi-Fi capability. There's a small bar for tea, coffee and light meals. For lunch and dinner, you'll use the restaurant car at the front of the train. Breakfast will be brought to your cabins by your steward.

The two cars behind the restaurant contain the second-class cabins. Each cabin has seats changed into beds at night, a simple basin for washing and a fold-down table. First-class cabins are at the back of the train. To reach them, pass through the lounge. Everyone may use it during the day, but after 6 p.m. it is exclusive to first-class passengers.

Right at the back is my office. If anyone needs me, use the phone in your cabin and press one. If I'm not there, tell your steward you need to see the manager.

The first part of our journey is from London to Paris through the Channel Tunnel. It takes just over an hour to the Tunnel, including a short passenger stop, then another three hours to Paris—about four hours altogether.

You won't need your passports until the Italian border, so keep them in the safe in your cabin. Ask your steward for a key.

Breakfast tomorrow is at about 7.30–7.45 in your cabins. Lunch is at 1 o'clock in the restaurant car and dinner is at 8, though please be at your table fifteen minutes earlier, at 7.45.

At the Italian border the train changes engines and gets a new crew. We'll take advantage of the stop to have a look around: the local market, a museum and a castle. We return in time for lunch.

You may eat your own food in your cabin, not in the restaurant or lounge. Keep cabin windows closed when you are away. Above all, don't get off the train until we reach the Italian border; other stops last only a few minutes.

Exercise 3

◉ Xem transcript

Welcome to Manham Port, where a thousand years of history are brought to life. The port lies on a bend in the River Avon. It developed here because this is the highest navigable point, a handy place to load and unload cargo to and from the sea.

All the family can enjoy a day out at Manham: visit our copper mine, see models of its machinery, have your photo taken in nineteenth-century costume, and experience at first hand how people lived, studied, worked and played. The port is located in beautiful, peaceful countryside on a bend in the great River Avon. It developed here because this is the highest navigable point of the Avon—boats can go no higher up this river—and it proved a handy place to load and unload cargo to and from the sea, which is over 23 miles away.

A small port was already established here when tin was discovered nearby about 900 years ago. It was not until the Industrial Revolution, when a tremendous need for metals of all kinds developed, that Manham expanded to become one of the busiest ports in the country. Prospectors began to look for other minerals, and by the end of the nineteenth century lead, copper, manganese and arsenic had been added to the cargoes leaving Manham.

In the early days, the ores had been smelted—or processed—in the same area where they were mined. As demand grew, smelting required huge factory furnaces to melt the metal from the rock. There was not enough coal in the local area, so rocks containing minerals had to be shipped long distances.

Sadly, in the twentieth century the great port declined and thousands of workers were forced to emigrate out of the area. Buildings at the port fell into disrepair and the place became almost forgotten. Then the Manham Trust was formed to conserve its historical resources. It organised scores of local volunteers to remove undergrowth and find the original outlines of the installations. It then brought in paid professionals to match installations with maps of the original port complex and set about reconstructing it. Today you can see the results of this ambitious programme of restoration. The intention is to return Manham Port to the condition it reached at its peak as the greatest copper port in the country.

Exercise 4

◉ Xem transcript

Longfield Park has a programme of activities throughout the year, and to give you a sample, this is what is happening in the next few days. On Monday you can learn about herbs and how they have been used over the centuries. You will start with four of our herb gardens, practise the technique of using them as colour dyes for cloth, and listen to an illustrated talk about their use in cooking and medicine.

Then on Wednesday you can join local experts to discover the variety of insects and birds that appear in the evening. We keep to a small number of people in the group, so if you want to go you will need to phone the park ranger a few days ahead. There is a small charge, which you should pay when you turn up.

I am sure you are all keen to help with the practical task of looking after the park, so on Saturday you can join a working party. You will have a choice of activities, from planting hedges to picking up litter, and can change from one to another when you feel like it. The rangers will be hard at work all day, but do join in even for just a short while. Make sure you wear something that you do not mind getting dirty or torn.

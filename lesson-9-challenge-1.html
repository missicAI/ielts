Exercise 2

◉ Xem transcript

Researcher: Hello, Joe. How much do you spend on electronic items a month?

Joe: I don't earn a lot, so after rent, bills and food, anything else is a luxury. I'd guess about five, maybe ten per cent of my monthly salary. First I look at Internet reviews, then ask my friends. Their opinions are probably more important than anything. A famous sportsman or actor in an advertisement can make a difference.

Most people avoid shops, but if you tell a shop assistant the Internet price, they often match it. So that's where I go. You get personal service and do not wait for delivery; I am impatient. I often want a new item in a shop window, especially if it is unusual.

Exercise 3

◉ Xem transcript

The mobile's purple and silver colour makes it eye-catching. Thanks to the oval design, it fits comfortably in the hand. The screen is dark and one extra warning noise is annoying. Joe wants a phone that is easy to operate and reliable rather than packed with games.

The digital radio sounds crisp and clear even at high volume, but it picks up few stations and its controls respond after a delay.

The laptop is compact and easy to use, but £900 is too expensive; Joe suggests about £400. Memory is adequate and the folding keyboard is clever. He would add a CD-ROM drive and a keyboard light.

Exercise 4

◉ Xem transcript

The Dinosaur Museum opens every day from 9 a.m. to 8 p.m., except Monday when it closes at 1.30 p.m. It is closed only on 25 December. School groups wait in the car park, where guides welcome and brief them. Allow at least 90 minutes: 15 minutes to get on and off the coach, 45 minutes for the tour and 30 minutes for after-tour activities. Cafés are available, and tables behind the museum are available for packed lunches.

Exercise 5

◉ Xem transcript

In the museum foyer, students check backpacks, books and lunch boxes into the cloakroom. The museum provides worksheets with questions and quizzes; students need pens. Cameras are allowed for project records. Food and drinks are not allowed inside.

Exercise 6

◉ Xem transcript

After the tour, students can watch continuous screenings of short dinosaur documentaries in the ground-floor theatrette. The activity room for models, drawing, painting and hunting dinosaur eggs is temporarily closed after storm damage. The IT centre has CD-ROM dinosaur games which teach about food, habitat and survival.

READING PASSAGE 1

THE BALLPOINT PEN

In 1945, 5,000 people crowded Gimbels Department Store after a full-page advertisement called the ballpoint pen “fantastic” and “miraculous”. All 10,000 expensive pens sold on the first day.

Yet John Loud had invented a reservoir-and-rolling-ball pen in 1888. It failed commercially because thin ink leaked and thick ink would not flow. In 1935 Hungarian newspaper editor Ladislas Biro, helped by his chemist brother, made a pen that did not leak when unused.

Argentinian president Augustine Justo invited Biro to set up a factory. The first 1943 pens had to be held vertically, so Biro redesigned the ball and sold the improved product in 1944. Milton Reynolds copied the design for the US market. Patrick Frawley improved it again and launched the successful Papermate in 1950.

LAND OF THE RISING SUN

A. Japanese pupils have higher and more consistent mathematical attainment than pupils in England and Wales despite similar education spending.

B. Lower-secondary schools cover ages 13–15. Classes are large, unstreamed and loyal; lessons last 50 minutes followed by a ten-minute break.

C. Traditional whole-class teaching uses small, logically developed textbooks supplied by Monbusho, which also sets the centralised curriculum.

D. Pupils first put homework solutions on the board and mark their own work. Teachers then explain new topics slowly with repetition, examples and individual textbook practice.

E. Mixed-ability classes progress together. Teachers, classmates, parents and private evening tuition help strugglers keep up.

F. Success reflects cultural respect for education, compulsory maths, hard work, accuracy, supportive classes and emphasis on self-improvement.

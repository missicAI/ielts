DAYLIGHT SAVING TIME

Daylight Saving Time dates back to ancient civilisations. Benjamin Franklin promoted daylight use in 1784; George Hudson proposed a two-hour shift in 1895; Germany first advanced clocks in 1916 to save wartime fuel. Use remained inconsistent until later national standards.

Benefits include evening outdoor recreation, lower domestic electricity use, better-lit peak traffic hours and increased tourism and hospitality revenue. Dairy farmers, however, report disrupted milking schedules.

Research also links the autumn clock change to risk for pedestrians, sleep loss to mood swings and poorer health, and darker mornings to street crime. Some studies report short-term rises in heart attacks and road accidents.

READING PASSAGE 2

THE LIFE OF SIR ISAAC NEWTON

A. Isaac Newton was born in 1643. After a failed attempt to make him a farmer, he studied at King's School and Trinity College. During the Great Plague he developed ideas on calculus, light, colour and gravity.

B. Back at Cambridge, he constructed the first reflecting telescope in 1668, joined the Royal Society and published on optics. He argued that white light comprises all spectral colours and that light consists of particles.

C. Encouraged by Edmond Halley, Newton organised his work into the 1687 Principia, establishing three laws of motion and universal gravity.

D. Newton became influential in public life and served as Warden and Master of the Royal Mint.

E. As Royal Society president he published Opticks, covering refraction, the colour spectrum, energy and electricity.

F. Newton and Leibniz disputed the origin of calculus; later researchers concluded both probably reached it independently.

G. Newton spent later years with his niece, died in 1727 and was buried in Westminster Abbey. His methods helped establish modern science.

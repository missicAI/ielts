READING PASSAGE 1

THE HONEY BADGER

The honey badger is an African and South-Asian mammal reputed to be one of the world's most fearless and aggressive animals despite its small size. It attacks lions, buffalo and snakes and recovers from severe injuries.

It is 75cm to one metre long; males are about twice the size of females. Grey and white stripes extend from head to tail, and it has pointed teeth and sharp front claws.

Its extremely varied meat diet includes beetles, lizards, birds, snakes, small crocodiles, foxes, antelope and wild cats. It locates prey mainly by smell and may dig fifty holes and travel 40 kilometres in a day. It also climbs trees.

It seeks nutritious bee eggs, not honey. Beekeepers set special traps to protect hives. A greater honeyguide bird guides the badger to hives, then bird and mammal share the brood.

READING PASSAGE 2

THE MEANING AND POWER OF SMELL

A. Smell evokes strong emotional responses. Odours associated with good or bad experiences can become agreeable or disagreeable for particular people.

B. Odours support social bonding. Infants recognise mothers by smell, and adults can distinguish clothing worn by marriage partners.

C. Although often undervalued, human smell is acute: noses recognise thousands of smells and tiny quantities.

D. Smell is elusive. Many languages lack specific vocabulary, and odours cannot be effectively captured or stored, so we rely on descriptions and recollections.

E. Scientific research has advanced, but questions remain about responses to odours and chemicals, body parts affected and objective measurement.

F. Smell is also cultural. An odour offensive in one culture may be acceptable in another; shared feelings about smell help distinguish cultures.
